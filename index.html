About

I am an Associate Professor at the Institute of Computing Technology (ICT), Chinese Academy of Sciences. Before joining ICT, I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science from the University of Rochester in 2023, advised by Prof. Yuhao Zhu. I also received my M.S. from the University of California, Santa Barbara and my B.E. from Beijing Institute of Technology.

My research is in computer architecture, with an emphasis on autonomous machines — robots, autonomous vehicles, and drones. I believe this is an exciting domain that may fundamentally change people's lives. I also work extensively on AI for chip design.

I am always looking for self-motivated students who love to explore new ideas and turn them into real systems. If you enjoy designing chips and building better software and hardware for next-generation robots, and know how to program, please write to me at ganyiming@ict.ac.cn. See Prospective Students for more details.

Research

I lead a research group at the Institute of Computing Technology, Chinese Academy of Sciences. We work on autonomous machines — an exciting area that may reshape the way we live. We hope to build autonomous machines that are more intelligent, more efficient, and more reliable. Our work spans new algorithms, new computer systems, and, hopefully, real robots.

AI for Chip Design

I believe the traditional chip design process is under a radical shift with the use of AI. With AI, we can move faster, while also needing to care more about the resilience of the design. Finding a way to efficiently use AI in chip design, quantify its ability, and improve that ability is extremely interesting to me.

  • AI for Chip Design Arena Hiring
  • Resilient AI for Chip Design

Efficient Embodied AI Systems

I think robots will have the ability to think. The goal of this direction is to enable robots to accomplish long and complex tasks in real time. There are two fundamental challenges. First, although large language models have shown the ability to accomplish long-horizon tasks, the gap between the output of language models and the real physical world is huge. Second, the scale of large models makes it impossible for robots to finish their decision chain in real time. We currently have several related projects.

  • Accelerator design for vision-language-action models
  • Humanoid robot control (ongoing)
  • Robotic GPU (ongoing)
  • Hardwired action expert accelerator (ongoing)

Reliability

My research focuses on reliability, which I believe is particularly crucial in a world increasingly populated by robots. Ensuring reliability involves making software robust against various attacks and hardware resilient to different hazards. We are currently working on two related projects.

  • Reliability for embodied AI systems (ongoing)
  • Software–hardware co-design for fault tolerance in wafer-scale chips Hiring

Prospective Students

Much of my mentoring philosophy comes from my advisor. It has worked well between us, and I hope it will work well between us too. Generally, I am looking for students who are self-motivated, love to explore new things, and love to turn their findings into real systems. The ideal mentoring relationship should be interactive: I can help you with what I know, and you can expand my horizons with what you know. I believe a good teacher should learn from their students, and a good student should teach their advisor.

What we are looking for

We welcome students from diverse backgrounds, even if your formal education did not involve extensive programming. We welcome students from areas such as physics, mathematics, and automation. It would be helpful if you are experienced in one or more of the following:

  • Solid programming skills (Python, C++)
  • Computer systems (e.g., computer architecture basics, FPGA experience, compiler techniques, programming language design)
  • Robotic applications (e.g., localization, planning, and control)
  • Computer vision (deep learning, reinforcement learning, foundation models)
  • Engineering mathematics (signal processing, dynamic systems, sparsity and compression, matrix methods, optimization)

How to get in touch

I encourage you to read our research directions first, then send me an email. Please explain why your interests align with ours and how you can contribute. Attaching a CV, transcripts, and any code or papers you are proud of is always helpful.
